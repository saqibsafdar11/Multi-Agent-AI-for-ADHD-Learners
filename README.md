# Multi-Agent AI as Distributed Cognitive Support for ADHD Learners

This research proposal designs and evaluates a multi-agent AI tutoring architecture for learners with ADHD. It was completed for the University of Leeds **OCOM5204M Data Mining and Text Analytics** module.

## Project overview

The proposal treats multi-agent AI as a form of distributed cognitive support. Four specialised roles - subject tutor, examiner, metacognition coach and progress tracker - are mapped to executive functions that ADHD can affect, including working memory, planning, self-monitoring and task switching.

The report combines:

- the centaur model of human-machine collaboration;
- Bloom's two-sigma problem and Cognitive Load Theory;
- a comparison of traditional intelligent tutoring systems, single-LLM tutors and multi-agent frameworks;
- a pilot study involving 182 pseudonymised tutoring messages over 4.8 hours;
- NLTK sentiment and vocabulary analysis;
- `textstat` readability analysis;
- error-pattern and pedagogical-feature classification; and
- a CRISP-DM plan for a larger comparative evaluation.

## Central research question

Can a coordinated team of specialised AI agents provide better cognitive scaffolding for ADHD learners than a single AI tutor or unaided revision?

## Proposed evaluation

The larger study would compare multi-agent support, single-agent tutoring and unsupported work using exam performance, self-correction, retention, engagement, pedagogical accuracy and token cost. The design retains teacher oversight and includes safeguarding, privacy and data-governance controls.
